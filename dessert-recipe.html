French Macarons

120 calories 90 minutes Beginner

Step-by-step cooking instructions

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (100g) superfine almond flour
  • 1 cup (130g) powdered sugar
  • 2 large egg whites, room temperature
  • ¼ cup (50g) granulated sugar
  • ½ teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt
  • 2 to 3 drops gel food coloring (optional)
  • ½ cup (115g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 ½ cups (190g) powdered sugar (for filling)
  • 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1 to 2 tablespoons heavy cream
  • Pinch of fine sea salt
  • ¼ cup raspberry jam (optional, for center)
  • Edible gold leaf, for garnish (optional)
Instructions
  1. Prepare two large baking sheets by lining them with silicone baking mats or heavy-duty parchment paper. If you are using parchment paper, dab a tiny amount of batter under the four corners to glue the paper flat against the tray so it does not curl or shift while piping.
  2. Place a fine-mesh sieve over a large mixing bowl and sift the almond flour and powdered sugar together. Discard any large almond pieces left in the sieve, then repeat the sifting process a second time. Taking the time to sift twice ensures your macaron shells turn out ultra-smooth and glossy.
  3. Wipe down a completely dry, grease-free glass or metal bowl with a drop of lemon juice or white vinegar to remove any lingering oil residue. Add the room-temperature egg whites and a pinch of salt, then beat with an electric mixer on medium speed until frothy bubbles form.
  4. Slowly add the granulated sugar, about one tablespoon at a time, while continuing to beat the egg whites on medium-high speed. Keep whipping until the meringue becomes thick, opaque, and forms stiff, glossy peaks that hold their shape straight up when you lift the beaters.
  5. Add the vanilla extract and two to three drops of gel food coloring to the meringue, beating briefly on low speed until just incorporated. Avoid using liquid food coloring, as extra moisture can ruin the delicate structure of the batter and prevent the shells from drying properly.
  6. Add half of the sifted dry ingredients into the meringue and gently fold them in using a flexible silicone spatula. Once mostly combined, add the remaining dry mixture and begin the macaronage technique by pressing the batter against the sides of the bowl to deflate excess air.
  7. Continue folding and scraping the bowl carefully until the batter reaches a smooth, thick consistency that flows off the spatula in a continuous ribbon, like warm lava. Test the texture by drizzling a figure eight into the bowl – if the ribbon melts back into the batter within ten seconds, stop mixing immediately.
  8. Fit a large piping bag with a medium round tip, twist the bottom to seal it, and place the bag upright inside a tall glass for easy filling. Transfer the batter into the bag, twist the top closed, and pipe uniform 1.5-inch circles onto your prepared baking sheets, spacing them about one inch apart.
  9. Hold each baking sheet firmly with both hands and rap it flat against the kitchen counter three to four times to bring trapped air bubbles to the surface. Use a clean toothpick to gently pop any remaining visible bubbles, then let the trays rest at room temperature for 30 to 45 minutes until the tops feel dry and no longer sticky to the touch.
  10. Meanwhile, preheat your oven to 300°F (150°C). Bake one tray at a time on the middle rack for 14 to 16 minutes, rotating the pan halfway through, until the macarons have developed ruffled feet and do not wobble when gently nudged. Let them cool completely on the baking sheet before peeling them off and sandwiching them with buttercream and jam.
